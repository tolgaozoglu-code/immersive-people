Scale has a bad reputation in creative fields, and it earned it. Most attempts to scale experiential work proceed by dilution: take what worked once, strip what made it expensive, and ship the residue. The result is the sameness audiences already sense in the global glut of projection shows. We think there is a different path, and it borrows its logic from an older art form.

## A production is a score, not a performance

Classical music solved scale centuries ago. A symphony is composed once, with total craft, as a score; it is then performed endlessly, in halls of every size, by orchestras of every character, without the composer present. The score is not a compromise between depth and reach. It is the technology that makes both possible.

Immersive productions can be built the same way. The core of a work, its narrative architecture, its sound world, its dramaturgy of peaks and endings, its visual language, is authored once and documented as rigorously as a score. The spatial realisation, which walls, which speaker count, which sightlines, is a performance of that score in a specific venue. Design the separation deliberately and a production stops being a site-specific miracle and becomes a repertoire piece.

## Venue-agnostic is a creative discipline, not a constraint

Designing for unknown rooms sounds like designing for the lowest common denominator. In practice it forces clarity. When you cannot rely on one architectural trick, the work must carry its power in structure: in story, pacing and sensory composition that survive translation. Cognitive research on narrative gives comfort here; transportation into a story depends far more on coherence and stakes than on resolution or square metres. The scenes audiences describe weeks later are rarely the most expensive ones.

Concretely, this means authoring sound as flexible spatial stems rather than fixed channel counts, defining visual composition in proportions rather than pixels, and writing the experience as a sequence of states a venue instantiates rather than a fixed floor plan.

## Documentation is where scale actually lives

The unglamorous truth: a production scales exactly as well as its documentation. Our delivery for a licensed work includes the narrative bible, the sensory score, adaptation rules stating what may change and what must not, and the rationale behind key decisions, because a partner who understands why will make better choices than one who only knows what. Localisation lives here too, and it is more than translation; a story crossing cultures needs its references re-anchored, which is editorial work the original team must anticipate.

## Teams that scale pair depth with craft

Every production we develop pairs a subject expert, someone with genuine standing in the material, with experience designers and technical realisers. The expert protects truth, the designers protect feeling, and the documentation protects both when neither is in the room. That last clause is the whole point. Scale is what happens to a work when its makers are absent, and building for that absence, honestly and in advance, is the difference between multiplying a work and diluting it.
